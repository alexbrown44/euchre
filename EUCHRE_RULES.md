# Euchre Rules

*As played in the **Lizard Peninsula Euchre League**, Cornwall.*

> **A short history.** Euchre most likely descends from *Jucker* (or *Juckerspiel*), a card game from Alsace, and it became hugely popular in 19th-century America. The Joker was first added to the pack for Euchre, around the 1850s, as a "best bower" above the Jacks. The game also took root in South West England, especially Cornwall and Devon. There the Joker is known as the **Benny**, and Cornwall is said to claim it, carried back and forth by Cornish miners who emigrated to North America. Today Euchre is still a pub-league game across Cornwall.

A trick-taking card game for **4 players in 2 partnerships**. Partners sit across from each other. The first team to reach the **agreed winning total** wins. Common totals are 10, 15 or 20. **Set the total before the game starts.**

---

## 1. The Deck

- **25 cards**: 9, 10, J, Q, K, A in each of the four suits (♠ ♥ ♦ ♣), plus **one Joker**.
- The Joker is called the **Benny** (also "Best Bower"). It is the highest card in the game.
- Some variants use a 32-card deck (adding 7s and 8s) or 28 cards (adding 8s). This document uses the 25-card deck with the Benny.

## 2. Card Rankings

### The Trump Suit (highest to lowest)
1. **Benny**: the Joker
2. **Right Bower**: Jack of the trump suit
3. **Left Bower**: Jack of the *other suit of the same color*
4. Ace of trump
5. King of trump
6. Queen of trump
7. 10 of trump
8. 9 of trump

The trump suit has **8 cards**.

> **Important:** The Benny is **always trump**, whatever suit is trump. It counts as a card of the trump suit when following suit. If trump is led, a player holding the Benny may follow with it. If they have no other trump, they must play it. If the Benny is led, it is a trump lead.

> **Important:** The Left Bower belongs to the trump suit for the whole hand. It does **not** count as its printed suit.
> Example: If **hearts** are trump, the J♦ is the Left Bower. It counts as a heart, and it is **not** a diamond during that hand.

| Trump | Right Bower | Left Bower |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| ♠ Spades | J♠ | J♣ |
| ♣ Clubs | J♣ | J♠ |
| ♥ Hearts | J♥ | J♦ |
| ♦ Diamonds | J♦ | J♥ |

### Non-Trump Suits (highest to lowest)
A, K, Q, J, 10, 9.
The suit of the same color as trump has only 5 cards, because its Jack became the Left Bower.

## 3. Choosing the First Dealer & Seating

- Partners sit opposite each other.
- Any fair method picks the first dealer. A common one: deal cards face up and the first player to get a Jack (any suit) deals.
- The deal passes **to the left (clockwise)** after each hand.

## 4. The Deal

- The dealer shuffles, and the player to the dealer's right may cut.
- Each player receives **5 cards**, dealt clockwise starting with the player to the dealer's left.
- The traditional pattern is batches of **3 then 2** (or 2 then 3), but any 5-card deal is allowed.
- The remaining **5 cards** form the **kitty**. The top card of the kitty is turned **face up**. This is the **upcard**.
- Whenever the dealer picks up the upcard (or the Benny), they **discard one card face down**. **No one**, including the dealer's partner, may see the discard, and it stays out of play for the hand. The rest of the kitty also stays face down and unseen.

### If the Benny Is Turned Up
The Joker has no suit, so it cannot be ordered up in the usual way. Instead:
- The **dealer names any suit as trump blind**, **before looking at their own cards**. There is no bidding.
- **Only after naming trump** does the dealer pick up their hand and the Benny. They may then **look at all six cards** and choose one to discard face down, as with a normal upcard.
- The dealer's team are the makers. **Only the dealer** may go alone.
- No other player may go alone, take over a loner, or defend alone in this hand.

## 5. Making Trump (Bidding)

### Round One: Order Up or Pass
Starting with the player to the dealer's left and going clockwise, each player either:
- **Passes**, or
- **Orders up** the upcard ("pick it up"). The upcard's suit becomes trump.

The **dealer speaks last**. If the other three players pass, the dealer may **pick up the upcard**, making its suit trump, or pass.

When an **opponent** orders up the upcard, or the **dealer** takes it, **the dealer adds it to their hand and discards one card face down** (into the kitty).

**If the dealer's partner orders up** (calling **"Up and Down"**), it is **always a loner**: one of the two partners plays alone and the other sits out. The upcard's suit is trump. The dealer has not yet had their turn, so the **dealer chooses** which of them plays:
- **Partner plays alone (default):** The dealer is dropped. They lay their cards face down, sit out the hand, and do **not** pick up the upcard.
- **Dealer takes over:** The dealer drops their partner and goes alone themselves. The dealer picks up the upcard and discards face down as normal, and their partner sits out.

### Round Two: Name a Suit
If all four players pass, the dealer turns the upcard face down. Starting again to the dealer's left, each player may:
- **Pass**, or
- **Name any suit except the one that was turned down** as trump.

The dealer again speaks last.

If all four players pass again, the hand is thrown in, all cards are collected, and the deal passes to the left.

### The Makers
The team that chose trump are the **makers**. The other team are the **defenders**.

## 6. Going Alone

- **Any player** may go alone when they make trump:
  - **Round one:** by ordering up the upcard and announcing "going alone."
  - **Round two:** by naming a suit and announcing "going alone."
- The maker must announce going alone **at the same moment** they order up or name trump, as one call (for example, "Pick it up, I'm going alone" or "Spades, alone"). They **cannot** decide to go alone afterwards, for example once the dealer has picked up, or after the first lead.
- This applies to the maker's own decision. Taking over a partner's loner and defending alone are decided later, on each player's own turn (see below).
- Their partner lays their cards face down and sits out the hand.
- **Taking over a partner's loner:** If your partner goes alone and you **have not yet had your turn in that round**, you may drop them and go alone yourself. Trump stays as your partner called it. Your partner then lays their cards face down and sits out instead.
- If the dealer's partner orders up in round one, it is always a loner. Either the partner plays alone, or the dealer takes over and plays alone (see Making Trump).
- **Turns continue after trump is made:** Once trump is made, the call carries on around the table. Each player who has **not yet had their turn in that round** still gets it, but **only** to take over a partner's loner, defend alone, or pass. The dealer, who speaks last, has the final say.
- **Defending alone:** Once trump is made, a defender may drop their partner and defend alone, **whether the maker is going alone or playing with their partner**. They may only do this **if they have not yet had their turn in that round**. Their partner then sits out.
  - **Both defenders, in turn order:** If the first defender to speak does not defend alone, the second may still do so on their turn. If the first defender **does** defend alone, the second may **take over** on their turn. The second defender then plays alone and the first sits out.
  - **Answering a lone defender:** If a defender goes alone, a maker's partner who has not yet had their turn may drop their partner and go alone in response.
  - **Up and Down:** When the dealer's partner calls Up and Down, the player on the dealer's right speaks next. They may defend alone **before** the dealer decides whether to take over.
  - **Dealer defending alone:** If an opponent orders up and the dealer defends alone, the dealer still picks up the upcard and discards face down.
  - **Dealer dropped:** Whenever the dealer is dropped (by Up and Down, or by their partner defending alone), the dealer turns in all their cards and does **not** pick up the upcard. It stays in the kitty.
- **Exception:** If the Benny is turned up, only the dealer may go alone (see The Deal).
- Going alone raises the reward for winning all 5 tricks (see Scoring).

## 7. Playing the Hand

1. The **player to the dealer's left** leads the first trick. (If that player is sitting out, the next active player clockwise leads. Players sitting out are skipped throughout the hand.)
2. Play goes **clockwise**. Each player plays one card **face up** to the center of the table. Players keep their hands hidden from everyone, including their partner.
3. **You must follow suit** if you can. Remember that the Left Bower counts as trump.
4. If you cannot follow suit, you may play **any card**, including trump.
5. The trick is won by:
   - The **highest trump** played, or, if no trump was played,
   - The **highest card of the suit led**.
6. The winning team gathers the completed trick and places it **face down** in front of them. Tricks may **not** be looked back at once turned over.
7. The winner of a trick leads the next one.
8. A hand has **5 tricks**.

## 8. Scoring

| Outcome | Points |
|---------|--------|
| Makers (both partners playing) win **3 or 4** tricks | **1** point to makers |
| Makers (both partners playing) win **all 5** tricks (a "march" or "sweep") | **2** points to makers |
| Maker going alone wins **3 or 4** tricks | **1** point to makers |
| Maker going alone wins **all 5** tricks | **4** points to makers |
| Makers win **fewer than 3** tricks ("**euchred**") | **2** points to defenders |
| A **lone defender** euchres the makers (whether the maker is alone or both partners are playing) | **4** points to defenders |

- The first team to reach the **agreed winning total** (for example 10, 15 or 20) wins. Set the total before the game starts.

### Traditional Score-Keeping
Each team commonly uses two low cards (often a 5 and a 6, or a 4 and a 6) to track points by covering or revealing the pips. This works for games to 10. For games to 15, use an 8 and a 7.

A **scoring board with pegs** (matchsticks work well) is also common and works for any winning total.

## 9. Common Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Upcard** | The face-up card from the kitty proposed as trump in round one |
| **Kitty** | The 5 undealt cards |
| **Benny** | The Joker, the highest trump card |
| **Order up** | Make the upcard's suit trump in round one. The dealer picks it up, unless the dealer's partner ordered it (a forced loner) |
| **Defending alone** | A defender playing without their partner, against either a loner or both makers |
| **Right Bower** | Jack of trump, the second-highest card |
| **Left Bower** | Jack of the same color as trump, the third-highest card |
| **Makers** | The team that chose trump |
| **Defenders** | The team that did not choose trump |
| **Euchre / Euchred** | When the makers fail to take 3 tricks |
| **March / Sweep** | Winning all 5 tricks |
| **Stopper** | A trick won by the defending team that stops the makers winning all 5 tricks. This holds them to 1 point instead of **2** (both partners playing) or **4** (going alone). |
| **Whitewash** | Losing a game without scoring a single point. That team has been "whitewashed." |
| **Die in the hole** | To lose the game while needing only **one more point** to win (for example, being stuck on 9 in a game to 10). When a team is in the hole, **both teams** have a stronger incentive to call trump, because the makers have the advantage of choosing trump. The team in the hole wins the game with any 3 tricks, and the other team wants to stop them making. |
| **Loner / Going alone** | Playing a hand without your partner |
| **Drop** | Dropping your partner to go alone. They lay their cards face down and sit out the hand. At the table, the player simply says "Drop." |
| **Up and Down** | The call the dealer's partner makes when ordering up in round one: the upcard's suit goes **up** as trump, and the dealer goes **down** (is dropped). The partner plays alone, unless the dealer takes over (see Making Trump). |
| **Next** | The suit of the same color as the turned-down upcard. Often a good second-round call for the **non-dealing team** ("make next"). See also *Against colour*. |
| **Against colour** | In round two, naming trump against the usual expectation: the **dealer's team** names a suit of the **same colour** as the turned-down card, or the **non-dealing team** names a suit of the **opposite colour**. The usual thinking is that if the upcard was turned down, the dealer's team is weak in that colour. See also *Next*. |
| **Trump** | The suit that beats all other suits for the hand |
| **The lead** | The right to play first in a trick. You have the lead when you win a trick (the first lead goes to the player on the dealer's left). Having the lead helps you **control the game**, because you choose which suit is played. |
| **Back hand** | The opposite of the lead: being the **last to play** in a trick. This can also be an advantage, because you see every other card before choosing yours. See also *The lead*. |
| **Let it go** | A player chooses **not** to try to win a trick, hoping their partner will win it instead. This saves their strong card for a later trick when the team still needs another one. It applies both when the partner is **already winning** the trick, and when the player plays **before** their partner and goes low, leaving the trick for the partner to win. |
| **Trumping in** | Playing a trump card on a trick when you can't follow the suit led |
| **Drawing / Drag** | Leading trump, often to pull the opponents' trump out of their hands. A well-timed one might earn a "Nice drag" from the table. |
| **Gone fishing** | A player on the makers' team plays a **low trump** while holding higher trumps. They hope to drag out the opponents' higher trumps, so their own remaining trumps become more powerful. See also *Drawing / Drag*. |
| **Sleeping** | A card that is in the kitty and out of play for the hand. Example: "The Benny was sleeping." |

## 10. Other Variants (Not Used in These Rules)

For reference only. Other groups play these, but they are **not** part of the rules above.

- **Stick the Dealer**: The dealer must call trump in round two.
- **No Ace, No Face, No Trump (Farmer's Hand)**: A player dealt a very weak hand may ask for a redeal.
- **Bigger decks**: 28- or 32-card decks.
- **Benny turned up, alternatives**: Some groups treat a turned-up Joker as a forced trump in a fixed suit (often hearts), or redeal. These rules use "dealer names trump blind."
- **Three-handed / Two-handed Euchre**: Variants for fewer players, using modified scoring.
